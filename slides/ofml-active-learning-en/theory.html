Theoretical statement and proofs

Consistency of \(C(\X)\)-valued Bayesian active learning and its specialization to OFML target active correction

This page is a fully reorganized version of the theory section. The old OFML transfer upper bound is removed from the main theorem, and active-learning consistency for a general \(C(\X)\)-valued Bayesian model is placed as the primary contribution. The main theorem shows that, for a well-specified \(C(\X)\)-valued posterior, noisy \(\IVR/\SUR\) makes the latent-function variance \(v_n\) vanish globally and drives the posterior mean \(\mu_n\) to uniform convergence to the true terminal function \(G\). We then construct OFML's fixed target protocol as the pushforward of a \(C(\X)\)-valued random function, and specialize the general theorem to target active correction. Overall architecture and the boundary of the claims

Two-stage structure: general theorem → OFML instance

The logical order is: first prove the general \(C(\X)\)-valued Bayesian active-learning theorem with a full proof, and then specialize it to OFML's target protocol via pushforward. The old transfer upper bound is not lifted to an independent global theorem. What remains on the transfer side is only the sufficient conditions (C1–C5) under which transfer can hold, the linear rank proposition, and the diagnosis of failure modes. The key point is the separation of transfer diagnostics and active correction: C1–C5 diagnose zero-shot transfer from source to target, but because target active correction observes the target terminal directly, the general theorem applies even when module identification fails, as long as the terminal response can be correctly treated as a \(C(\X)\)-valued model.

Skeleton of the proof (noisy main theorem)

The most nontrivial step is \(\sup_x\DeltaIVR_n(x)\to0\ \Rightarrow\ \norm{v_n}_\infty\to0\). If \(v_n\) stays positive at some point, then by posterior mean-square equicontinuity the covariance \(K_n(u,x)\) also stays positive in a neighborhood, and since the full-support measure assigns uniform mass to that neighborhood, the one-step expected reduction stays positive — which contradicts \(\sup_x\DeltaIVR_n\to0\).

\[ \begin{gathered} \mu_n\to\mu_\infty,\quad v_n\to v_\infty \ \text{ in } C(\X) \ \Downarrow\ H_n=\textstyle\int v_n\,d\lambda \ \text{ is a nonnegative supermartingale} \ \Downarrow\ \sup_x\DeltaIVR_n(x)\to0 \ \Downarrow\ \norm{v_n}_\infty\to0 \ (\text{linear-predictor lower bound}+\text{full support}) \ \Downarrow\ G=\mu_\infty,\quad \norm{\mu_n-G}_\infty\to0. \end{gathered} \]

1. General \(C(\X)\)-valued Bayesian model

We treat the unknown terminal function as \(G:\Omega\to C(\X)\). \(\X\) is a compact metric space, and the posterior is not restricted to a Gaussian process. The goal is to show that the posterior mean \(\mu_n\) converges uniformly to the true function \(G\), i.e. \(\norm{\mu_n-G}_\infty\to0\).

Assumption1Input space and latent function

Let \((\Omega,\F,\Pp)\) be a probability space and \((\X,d)\) a nonempty compact metric space. \(C(\X)\) is a separable Banach space under the uniform norm \(\norm{g}_\infty=\sup_{x}\abs{g(x)}\), and its Borel space is a standard Borel space. The unknown latent function \(G:\Omega\to C(\X)\) is Borel measurable and satisfies \(\E\norm{G}_\infty^2<\infty\). The history \((\Hh_n)_{n\ge0}\) is an increasing sequence of \(\sigma\)-algebras, with \(\Hh_\infty=\sigma(\bigcup_n\Hh_n)\).

Assumption2Correct posterior distribution

For each \(n\in\N\cup\{\infty\}\), let \(\Pi_n=\Law(G\mid\Hh_n)\) be a version of the regular conditional distribution of \(G\) given \(\Hh_n\).

Posterior moments

We define the posterior mean, raw second moment, covariance kernel, and latent variance as follows.

\[ \mu_n=\E[G\mid\Hh_n],\quad M_n=\E[G\tensor G\mid\Hh_n],\quad K_n(x,u)=M_n(x,u)-\mu_n(x)\mu_n(u),\quad v_n(x)=K_n(x,x). \]

In the noisy setting, what should vanish is not the observation-predictive variance (\(=v_n(x)+\) noise variance, which does not go to zero under fixed noise) but the latent-function variance \(v_n(x)=\Var(G(x)\mid\Hh_n)\). We set the residual uncertainty as \(H_n=\int_\X v_n(u)\,\lambda(du)\).

Lemma1Identification of point evaluation and posterior variance

Under Assumption 1 and Assumption 2, one can take a version for which \(\mu_n(x)=\E[G(x)\mid\Hh_n]\) and \(M_n(x,u)=\E[G(x)G(u)\mid\Hh_n]\) hold. Hence \(v_n(x)=\Var(G(x)\mid\Hh_n)\) and \(K_n(x,u)=\Cov(G(x),G(u)\mid\Hh_n)\).

Show proof

The point evaluation \(\delta_x(g)=g(x)\) is a continuous linear map. By the commutativity of the Bochner conditional expectation with continuous linear maps, \(\delta_x(\mu_n)=\E[G(x)\mid\Hh_n]\). Similarly, applying the \((x,u)\) point evaluation on \(C(\X\times\X)\) gives \(M_n(x,u)=\E[G(x)G(u)\mid\Hh_n]\). Centering shows that \(v_n,K_n\) coincide with the pointwise conditional variance and covariance. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Lemma2Closed Banach-valued Lévy upward convergence

Let \(E\) be a separable Banach space, \(Z\in L^1(\Omega;E)\), and let \(Z_n=\E[Z\mid\F_n]\) and \(Z_\infty=\E[Z\mid\F_\infty]\) be Bochner conditional expectations. Then \(\norm{Z_n-Z_\infty}_E\to0\) almost surely and \(\E\norm{Z_n-Z_\infty}_E\to0\).

Show proof

By the tower rule, \(Z_n=\E[Z_\infty\mid\F_n]\). Since \(E\) is separable, for any \(\delta>0\) there is an \(\F_N\)-measurable finite-valued simple function \(S\) with \(\E\norm{Z_\infty-S}_E<\delta\). For \(n\ge N\), conditional Jensen gives \(\E\norm{Z_n-Z_\infty}_E\le2\E\norm{Z_\infty-S}_E<2\delta\) (\(L^1\) convergence). For almost-sure convergence, choosing \(\E\norm{Z_\infty-S_k}_E\le2^{-3k}\) and applying Doob's weak-type maximal inequality to \(\norm{Z_n-S_k}_E\le\E[\norm{Z_\infty-S_k}_E\mid\F_n]\) (\(\Pp(\sup_{n\ge N_k}\norm{Z_n-S_k}_E>2^{-k})\le2^{-2k}\)) together with Borel–Cantelli yields the claim. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Proposition1Uniform convergence of the common posterior moments

Under Assumption 1 and Assumption 2, almost surely

\[ \mu_n\to\mu_\infty=\E[G\mid\Hh_\infty]\ \text{ in } C(\X),\qquad M_n\to M_\infty\ \text{ in } C(\X\times\X), \]

hence \(K_n\to K_\infty\) and \(v_n\to v_\infty\) hold in \(C(\X)\).

Show proof

Applying Lemma 2 to \(G\in L^1(\Omega;C(\X))\) with \(E=C(\X)\) gives \(\mu_n\to\mu_\infty\). Since \(Q(g)(x,u)=g(x)g(u)\) satisfies \(\norm{Q(g)}=\norm{g}_\infty^2\), we have \(G\tensor G\in L^1(\Omega;C(\X\times\X))\), and the same lemma gives \(M_n\to M_\infty\). Uniform convergence of \(K_n=M_n-\mu_n\tensor\mu_n\) and restriction to the diagonal give \(v_n\to v_\infty\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

2. Warm-up example: noiseless \(\MaxVar\)

We assume noiseless observations and treat \(\MaxVar\) as an introduction to the proof structure. The main purpose is to make explicit the structure of "closing variance vanishing from common posterior-moment convergence."

Assumption3Noiseless observation and \(\MaxVar\) approximate maximization

At each time \(n\), \(X_{n+1}\) is \(\Hh_n\)-measurable, the observation is \(Y_{n+1}=G(X_{n+1})\), and \(\Hh_{n+1}=\sigma(\Hh_n,X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})\). Moreover, there is a nonnegative error \(\eta_n^{\MaxVar}\ge0\) with \(v_n(X_{n+1})\ge\sup_x v_n(x)-\eta_n^{\MaxVar}\) and \(\eta_n^{\MaxVar}\to0\) almost surely.

Theorem1Consistency of noiseless \(\MaxVar\) function learning

Under Assumption 1, Assumption 2, and Assumption 3, almost surely \(\norm{v_n}_\infty\to0\), \(G=\mu_\infty\) in \(C(\X)\), and \(\norm{\mu_n-G}_\infty\to0\).

Show proof

In the noiseless case, \(G(X_{n+1})=Y_{n+1}\) is \(\Hh_{n+1}\)-measurable, so \(v_{n+1}(X_{n+1})=0\). By Proposition 1, \(\norm{v_n-v_{n+1}}_\infty\to0\), hence \(v_n(X_{n+1})\to0\). By approximate maximization, \(\norm{v_n}_\infty\le v_n(X_{n+1})+\eta_n^{\MaxVar}\to0\), hence \(v_\infty\equiv0\). On a countable dense set \(G(q_j)=\mu_\infty(q_j)\), and by continuity \(G=\mu_\infty\). Finally \(\norm{\mu_n-G}_\infty=\norm{\mu_n-\mu_\infty}_\infty\to0\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Its role as a warm-up example

The proof for \(\MaxVar\) relies on the noiseless interpolation property \(v_{n+1}(X_{n+1})=0\); it is concise but cannot be used under fixed noise. In the noisy main theorem we switch to the supermartingale structure of the residual uncertainty \(H_n\).

3. Main theorem: noisy \(\IVR/\SUR\)

Assumption4Additive, conditionally unbiased, finite-conditional-variance observation

\(X_{n+1}\) is \(\Hh_n\)-measurable and the observation is \(Y_{n+1}=G(X_{n+1})+\eps_{n+1}\). The noise is conditionally unbiased, \(\E[\eps_{n+1}\mid G,\Hh_n,X_{n+1}]=0\), and for a known constant \(0\le\tau^2<\infty\), \(\Var(\eps_{n+1}\mid G,\Hh_n,X_{n+1})\le\tau^2\).

The proof uses only conditional unbiasedness and the finite variance bound; sub-Gaussianity is not assumed (\(\tau^2=0\) includes the noiseless case). Sub-Gaussianity is an optional strengthening condition for finite-sample concentration and for connecting to the experimental report.

Assumption5Evaluation measure with full support

\(\lambda\) is a finite Borel measure on \(\X\) with \(\supp(\lambda)=\X\).

Assumption6Posterior mean-square equicontinuity

On an event of probability \(1\), there is a nondecreasing function \(\rho\) (\(\rho(r)\to0,\ r\downarrow0\)) such that for all \(n\) and \(x,u\in\X\), \(\E[(G(u)-G(x))^2\mid\Hh_n]\le\rho(d(u,x))^2\).

Fantasy observation kernel and the noisy \(\IVR\) acquisition function

For each \(n\), we measurably fix the regular posterior kernel \(\Pi_n\), the fantasy noise kernel \(R_n(\omega,x,g,de)\) (a measurable version of the regular conditional distribution of the actual noise), the predictive kernel \(Q_n^x\) of the fantasy observation \(Y_x=g(x)+e\), and the fantasy posterior kernel \(\Pi_n^{x,y}\) (measurability follows from the group of assumptions above and standard Borel-ness). For the fantasy posterior variance \(v_n^{x,y}(u)\) and the fantasy residual uncertainty \(H_{n+1}^{x,y}=\int v_n^{x,y}\,d\lambda\), we define the acquisition function at a candidate point \(x\) by

\[ \DeltaIVR_n(x)=H_n-\int_\R H_{n+1}^{x,y}\,Q_n^x(dy) \]

Approximate maximization rule: \(x\mapsto\DeltaIVR_n(x)\) has an upper-semicontinuous version, and the \(\Hh_n\)-measurable query point \(X_{n+1}\) satisfies \(\DeltaIVR_n(X_{n+1})\ge\sup_x\DeltaIVR_n(x)-\eta_n^{\IVR}\) with \(\eta_n^{\IVR}\to0\) (measurable selection follows from the compactness of \(\X\) and upper semicontinuity).

Lemma3Supermartingale property of the residual uncertainty and its agreement with the acquisition function

\(H_n=\int_\X v_n\,d\lambda\) is a nonnegative integrable supermartingale, \(\E[H_{n+1}\mid\Hh_n]\le H_n\). At the actually selected point, \(D_n:=H_n-\E[H_{n+1}\mid\Hh_n]=\DeltaIVR_n(X_{n+1})\), and \(\sum_n\E[D_n]\le\E[H_0]<\infty\). Hence, under approximate maximization, \(\sup_x\DeltaIVR_n(x)\to0\) almost surely.

Show proof

By the law of total variance, \(v_n(u)=\E[v_{n+1}(u)\mid\Hh_n]+\Var(\E[G(u)\mid\Hh_{n+1}]\mid\Hh_n)\), hence \(\E[v_{n+1}(u)\mid\Hh_n]\le v_n(u)\). Conditional Tonelli gives \(\E[H_{n+1}\mid\Hh_n]\le H_n\). Since the fantasy kernel agrees with the actual update kernel, \(\E[H_{n+1}\mid\Hh_n]=\int H_{n+1}^{X_{n+1},y}Q_n^{X_{n+1}}(dy)\), so \(D_n=\DeltaIVR_n(X_{n+1})\). From the telescoping sum of the nonnegative supermartingale, \(\sum_{k\le N}\E[D_k]=\E[H_0]-\E[H_{N+1}]\le\E[H_0]\), we get \(D_n\to0\) a.s. With \(\DeltaIVR_n\ge0\) and approximate maximization, \(0\le\sup_x\DeltaIVR_n(x)\le\DeltaIVR_n(X_{n+1})+\eta_n^{\IVR}\to0\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Lemma4Linear-predictor lower bound for noisy \(\IVR\)

For any \(x\in\X\), \(\displaystyle \DeltaIVR_n(x)\ge\int_\X\frac{K_n(u,x)^2}{v_n(x)+\tau^2}\,\lambda(du)\) (when \(v_n(x)+\tau^2=0\), read the integrand as \(0\)).

Show proof

\(Y_x=G(x)+\eps_x\). By conditional unbiasedness, \(\Covn(G(u),Y_x)=K_n(u,x)\) and \(\Varn(Y_x)\le v_n(x)+\tau^2\). By the law of total variance, \(\DeltaIVR_n(x)=\int\Varn(\E[G(u)\mid\Hh_n,Y_x])\,\lambda(du)\). Since \(\E[\,\cdot\mid\Hh_n,Y_x]\) is the orthogonal projection in \(L^2\) onto \(\sigma(Y_x)\), \(\Varn(\E[G(u)\mid\Hh_n,Y_x])\ge\Covn(G(u),Y_x)^2/\Varn(Y_x)\ge K_n(u,x)^2/(v_n(x)+\tau^2)\). Integrating gives the conclusion. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Auxiliary estimates (neighborhood lower bound and small-ball mass)

Neighborhood lower bound for the covariance: From Assumption 6 and conditional Cauchy–Schwarz, \(\abs{K_n(u,x)-v_n(x)}\le\rho(d(u,x))\sqrt{\norm{v_n}_\infty}\). Uniform small-ball mass: If \(\lambda\) has full support and the space is compact, then for any \(r>0\), \(m(r)=\inf_x\lambda(B(x,r))>0\) (by a finite cover).

Proposition2Global variance vanishing via noisy \(\IVR/\SUR\)

Under Assumption 1–Assumption 6 (and a measurable fantasy kernel and approximate maximization), \(\norm{v_n}_\infty\to0\) almost surely.

Show proof

By contradiction. There exist \(\delta>0\), a subsequence \((n_j)\), and maximizers \(x_{n_j}\) with \(v_{n_j}(x_{n_j})=\norm{v_{n_j}}_\infty\ge\delta\). Let \(V_\star=\sup_n\norm{v_n}_\infty<\infty\). From the neighborhood lower bound, choosing a small \(r\) with \(\rho(r)\sqrt{V_\star}\le\delta/2\) gives \(K_{n_j}(u,x_{n_j})\ge\delta/2\) for \(d(u,x_{n_j})Lemma 4,

\[ \DeltaIVR_{n_j}(x_{n_j})\ge\int_{B(x_{n_j},r)}\frac{K_{n_j}(u,x_{n_j})^2}{v_{n_j}(x_{n_j})+\tau^2}\lambda(du)\ge\frac{\delta^2}{4(V_\star+\tau^2)}\,m(r)>0 \]

holds infinitely often, contradicting \(\sup_x\DeltaIVR_n(x)\to0\) from Lemma 3. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Theorem2Consistency of function learning via noisy \(\IVR/\SUR\) (main theorem)

Under Assumption 1–Assumption 6, almost surely

\[ G=\mu_\infty\ \text{ in } C(\X),\qquad \norm{\mu_n-G}_\infty\to0. \]
Show proof

By Proposition 2, \(\norm{v_n}_\infty\to0\), and by Proposition 1, \(v_\infty\equiv0\). On a countable dense set, \(\Var(G(q_j)\mid\Hh_\infty)=0\), i.e. \(G(q_j)=\mu_\infty(q_j)\) a.s., and by continuity \(G=\mu_\infty\). Since \(\mu_n\to\mu_\infty\) uniformly, \(\norm{\mu_n-G}_\infty\to0\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

4. Model-level sufficient conditions

Assumption 6 looks like a posterior-level condition, but it can be verified from the model's sample-path modulus of continuity. Its role is to reduce identifiability to sample-path regularity rather than positing it as an assumption close to the conclusion.

Assumption7Model-level square-integrable modulus of continuity

There exist a nondecreasing function \(\omega\) (\(\omega(r)\to0\)) and a nonnegative random variable \(\Lambda\) (\(\E[\Lambda^2]<\infty\)) such that, with probability \(1\), for all \(x,u\), \(\abs{G(u)-G(x)}\le\Lambda\,\omega(d(u,x))\).

Proposition3Model-level modulus of continuity → posterior mean-square equicontinuity

Under Assumption 7, Assumption 6 holds.

Show proof

Taking the conditional expectation of \((G(u)-G(x))^2\le\Lambda^2\omega(d(u,x))^2\) gives \(\E[(G(u)-G(x))^2\mid\Hh_n]\le\omega(d(u,x))^2\,\E[\Lambda^2\mid\Hh_n]\). \(M_n^\Lambda=\E[\Lambda^2\mid\Hh_n]\) is a closed nonnegative scalar martingale that converges a.s., with \(C_\Lambda=\sup_n M_n^\Lambda<\infty\) a.s. Taking \(\rho(r)=\sqrt{C_\Lambda}\,\omega(r)\) gives the conclusion. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Verification strategy for a single GP, GPN, and BNN

A single GP follows from the kernel's modulus of continuity and sample-path regularity. A GP function network builds up from each node's sample-path modulus of continuity and the Lipschitz stability of composition. For a BNN, one verifies the square-integrability of the random Lipschitz constant \(\Lambda\) from the Lipschitz continuity of the activations, the second moment of the weight prior, and the compactness of the input domain.

5. Approximate inference and acquisition-function error

Diagnostic quantities to be separated

The main theorem assumes a correct posterior and that the approximate-maximization error of the true acquisition function satisfies \(\eta_n^{\IVR}\to0\). In implementations (MCMC, SMC, VI, Laplace, deep ensembles), the following are diagnosed separately.

\[ a_n=\norm{\widehat\mu_n-\mu_n}_\infty,\quad b_n=\norm{\widehat v_n-v_n}_\infty,\quad d_{\Delta,n}=\norm{\widehat\DeltaIVR_n-\DeltaIVR_n}_\infty,\quad \eta_n^{\mathrm{opt}}=\sup_x\widehat\DeltaIVR_n(x)-\widehat\DeltaIVR_n(\widehat X_{n+1}). \]

If \(d_{\Delta,n}\to0\) and \(\eta_n^{\mathrm{opt}}\to0\), then it can be controlled by \(\eta_n^{\IVR}\le2d_{\Delta,n}+\eta_n^{\mathrm{opt}}\to0\). VI with a fixed approximating family, fixed Laplace, and a fixed-size ensemble do not in general guarantee \(a_n,b_n,d_{\Delta,n}\to0\). Only growing-budget MCMC/SMC, approximating families with increasing capacity, or approximate inference whose consistency can be verified on function space are consistent with the theoretical conditions.

6. Specialization to OFML target active correction

We specialize the general \(C(\X)\) theorem to OFML. The target is a fixed protocol \(p_\star\) and a single terminal observer \(q\in\Obs(p_\star)\). We restrict the transfer side to diagnostic conditions, and obtain convergence from the direct active observation of the target terminal.

Transfer diagnostics (the sufficient conditions retained) and the claim that is removed

The old-style global transfer upper bound that guarantees the target response from source protocols alone is not adopted as the main theorem. For the source-side empirical error \(\dtrain(\theta,\theta')=\sup_{p\in\Psrc}\sup_{x}\norm{\Eval(p;\theta;x)-\Eval(p;\theta';x)}\) and the target terminal error \(d_{\star,q}(\theta,\theta')=\sup_x\norm{\Eval_q(p_\star;\theta;x)-\Eval_q(p_\star;\theta';x)}\), the following conditions serve to diagnose \(d_{\star,q}\) from source information.

Assumption8Sufficient conditions C1–C5 for OFML transfer diagnostics (optional C6)

C1 (vocabulary) All target node types are contained in the same module vocabulary as the source. C2 (semantics) The same type name carries the same operational meaning. C3 (coverage) The local input \(\Ucal_j^\star\) received by type \(j\) in the target is covered by the source probe's \(\Ucal_j^{\mathrm{src}}\) up to a residual \(\rho_j\ge0\). C4 (target-relevant identifiability) There exist a projection \(\Pi_{j,q}\), a constant \(K_j<\infty\), and an identification residual \(r_{j,q}^{\mathrm{id}}\ge0\) such that \(\norm{\Pi_{j,q}F_j^\theta(z)-\Pi_{j,q}F_j^{\theta'}(z)}\le K_j\dtrain(\theta,\theta')+r_{j,q}^{\mathrm{id}}+\rho_j\). C5 (Lipschitz stability) Each module is Lipschitz in its local input, so that local perturbations propagate to the terminal in a bounded way. C6 (optional realizability) The reference target response is representable within the class (only in the well-specified version).

Knowledge-transfer upper bound as a diagnostic (the old main theorem demoted)

Summing the per-module inequality of C4 along the DAG's downstream Lipschitz amplification \(\Lambda_v(p_\star,q)\), one formally obtains the following

\[ d_{\star,q}(H,F^\dagger)\ \le\ C_{\mathrm{KT}}(p_\star,q)\,\dtrain(H,F^\dagger)+R_{\mathrm{id/cov}}(p_\star,q) \]

knowledge-transfer upper bound (with \(C_{\mathrm{KT}}=\sum_v\Lambda_v K_{\tau(v)}\), \(R_{\mathrm{id/cov}}=\sum_v\Lambda_v(r^{\mathrm{id}}_{\tau(v),q}+\rho_{\tau(v)})\)). In this paper we do not lift this to an independent transfer theorem; we treat it only as a diagnostic of whether zero-shot transfer can be expected. Consistency in the case where an identification residual \(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\) or a coverage residual \(\rho>0\) remains is left to the target active correction below.

Linear characterization

For an additive affine model, \(\Eval(p;\theta;x)=\varphi_{p,x}^\top\theta\). Let \(\Phi\) be the sensitivity operator of the source probe and \(\Psi\) that of the target terminal.

Proposition4Identifiability of additive affine OFML (rank condition)

(i) \(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ker\Phi\subseteq\ker\Psi\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \row(\Psi)\subseteq\row(\Phi)\). (ii) In this case the minimal C4 constant is \(K=\sup_{\delta\theta\notin\ker\Phi}\frac{\norm{\Psi\delta\theta}}{\norm{\Phi\delta\theta}}\le\frac{\norm{\Psi}_2}{\sigma_{\min}^{+}(\Phi)}\). (iii) If the coefficients are unique under a full-rank probe, then the coverage residual \(\rho=0\) in the corresponding directions. (iv) If \(\Phi\) has full column rank and the empirical error is \(\le\delta\), then \(\norm{\widehat\theta-\theta^\dagger}\le\delta/\sigma_{\min}(\Phi)\).

Show proof

(i) A necessary condition for a finite dominating constant \(K\) is \(\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker\Psi\). Conversely, if it holds, then \(\Psi\) induces a bounded operator on the quotient space \(\ThetaSpace/\ker\Phi\) and \(K<\infty\). By linear algebra this is equivalent to the \(\row\) condition. (ii) Projecting onto \((\ker\Phi)^\perp\), \(\norm{\Phi\delta\theta}\ge\sigma_{\min}^{+}(\Phi)\norm{\delta\theta}\) and \(\norm{\Psi\delta\theta}\le\norm{\Psi}_2\norm{\delta\theta}\). (iii) If the coefficients are unique under a full-rank probe, then the target values are also determined from the same coefficients. (iv) Standard least-squares perturbation estimate. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

The three-body \(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\) failure is Fig 1b (s7), and the identifiability rank audit is the Φ/Ψ audit (Claim 1, 1-B).

Failure modes: three-body interaction and excess latent dimension

Three-body interaction: In color-water mixing, if a three-body term \(\gamma\,rgb\) is allowed, then for single-color and two-color probes \(rgb=0\) always, so \(\gamma\in\ker\Phi\). If \(rgb\ne0\) for a three-color target, then \(\gamma\notin\ker\Psi\). Hence \(\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker\Psi\) is violated and \(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\) (arising from the kernel of the probe design, independent of the presence or absence of nonlinearity). Excess latent dimension: If a gauge is unobservable in both the source and the target, it enters \(\ker\Phi\cap\ker\Psi\) and is harmless. A direction that is unobservable in the source but matters for the target enters \(\ker\Phi\setminus\ker\Psi\) and \(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\). Excess capacity is organized as a problem of target-relevant identifiability rather than of capacity itself.

Pushforward and the Lipschitz bridge

Lemma5\(C(\Xtar)\) pushforward of the OFML target posterior

Let \(\Xtar=\X_{p_\star}\) be compact metric. Suppose (a) \(x\mapsto\Eval_q(p_\star;\theta;x)\) is continuous for each \(\theta\), (b) \((\theta,x)\mapsto\Eval_q(p_\star;\theta;x)\) is measurable, and (c) an envelope \(B(\theta)\) satisfies \(\sup_x\abs{\Eval_q(p_\star;\theta;x)}\le B(\theta)\) and \(\E[B(\theta)^2]<\infty\). Then \(T_{p_\star,q}(\theta)(x)=\Eval_q(p_\star;\theta;x)\) is measurable and \(G=T_{p_\star,q}(\theta)\) is a \(C(\Xtar)\)-valued square-integrable random variable. Moreover, \(\Pi_n^G=(T_{p_\star,q})_\#\Pi_n^\theta=\Law(G\mid\Hh_n^{\mathrm{tar}})\).

Show proof

Point evaluations on a countable dense set generate the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra of \(C(\Xtar)\), and since each is pointwise measurable by (b), \(T_{p_\star,q}\) is measurable. By (c), \(\E\norm{G}_\infty^2\le\E[B(\theta)^2]<\infty\). For any Borel \(A\), \((T_{p_\star,q})_\#\Pi_n^\theta(A)=\Pp(G\in A\mid\Hh_n^{\mathrm{tar}})\), so the pushforward is a correct posterior. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Lemma6OFML Lipschitz bridge

Suppose the local-input injection \(\iota_v\) of each node \(v\) is Lipschitz in the design input (constant \(c_v\)), and each module \(F_{\tau(v)}^\theta\) is Lipschitz in its local input (constant \(L_{\tau(v)}(\theta)\)). If \(\Lambda_{p_\star,q}(\theta)=\sum_{v\preceq q}c_v\sum_{\pi:v\to q}\prod_{w\in\pi}L_{\tau(w)}(\theta)\) is finite and \(\E[\Lambda_{p_\star,q}(\theta)^2]<\infty\), then \(G_\theta(x)=\Eval_q(p_\star;\theta;x)\) satisfies \(\abs{G_\theta(u)-G_\theta(x)}\le\Lambda_{p_\star,q}(\theta)\,d(u,x)\). Hence Assumption 7 (\(\omega(r)=r\)) holds, and by Proposition 3, Assumption 6 follows.

Show proof

Induct on the state difference \(e_v=\norm{s_v^\theta(u)-s_v^\theta(x)}\) in the topological order of the DAG. With no parents, \(e_v\le L_{\tau(v)}c_v d(u,x)\); with parents, \(e_v\le L_{\tau(v)}(c_v d(u,x)+\sum_{a\in\Pa(v)}e_a)\). Expanding, the design-input-induced difference at each node is amplified by the product of Lipschitz constants along each path \(v\to q\), giving \(e_q\le\Lambda_{p_\star,q}(\theta)d(u,x)\). By square-integrability, \(\E[\Lambda^2]<\infty\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Bounded version and independent version

If one imposes \(L_j(\theta)\le\bar L_j\) via a spectral-norm constraint, bounded activations, and weight clipping, then \(\Lambda_{p_\star,q}\) is deterministically bounded. For independent random Lipschitz constants, one directly verifies \(\E[\prod_j L_j(\theta)^2]<\infty\).

Consistency of target active correction

Theorem3Terminal-response consistency of OFML target active correction

Consider a fixed target protocol \(p_\star\), a single terminal \(q\), and compact \(\Xtar\). Assume (i) the pushforward posterior is correct under the conditions of Lemma 5, (ii) the target observations satisfy Assumption 4, (iii) \(\lambda\) has full support on \(\Xtar\) (Assumption 5), (iv) a measurable fantasy kernel, (v) \(\IVR/\SUR\) approximate maximization, and (vi) the conditions of Lemma 6. Then \(\norm{v_n}_\infty\to0\) and \(\norm{\mu_n-G}_\infty\to0\) a.s. In particular, if well-specified \(f_{p_\star}^{\dagger}=G\), then \(\norm{\mu_n-f_{p_\star}^{\dagger}}_\infty\to0\) a.s.

Show proof

Discharge Assumption 1 and Assumption 2 via Lemma 5, Assumption 4 and Assumption 5 together with a measurable kernel and approximate maximization via (ii)–(v), and Assumption 6 via Lemma 6 + Proposition 3. Applying the main theorem (Theorem 2) with \(\X=\Xtar\) gives the conclusion. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Proposition5Even if transfer identification fails, target active correction can rescue it

Suppose that, in the sense of Proposition 4, \(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\) remains, so that zero-shot transfer from the source alone has a target residual. Even so, if the assumptions of Theorem 3 are satisfied and the target terminal \(f_{p_\star}^{\dagger}\) is well-specified, then \(\norm{\mu_n-f_{p_\star}^{\dagger}}_\infty\to0\) a.s.

Show proof

\(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\) means that the target-relevant directions cannot be identified from the source protocols alone, but Theorem 3 does not assume source-only transfer. The source data \(\Dsrc\) merely initializes the posterior as the initial history \(\Hh_0^{\mathrm{tar}}\), after which target terminal measurements are added by the \(\IVR/\SUR\) rule with full-support \(\lambda\). Applying it gives the conclusion. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Depth limit and the motivation for intermediate observation

For a target of chain depth \(D\), the bridge coefficient typically contains \(\Lambda_{p_\star,q}(\theta)\asymp\prod_{k=1}^{D}L_k(\theta)\). If \(\E[\prod_k L_k(\theta)^2]\) grows with depth, the \(\rho\) coefficient in Assumption 6 becomes looser (not a counterexample, but an issue of the proof constants that can make variance collapse appear slow under a finite budget). Moreover, if \(d_{\Delta,n}\not\to0\) under finite-particle Monte Carlo, the approximate-maximization condition cannot be met, and Theorem 3 does not apply to the implemented policy. Because the estimation of the sample covariance and the fantasy-observation posterior is unstable under deep non-Gaussian pushforwards, intermediate observation and partial evaluation of internal nodes have a design motivation for improving both \(\Lambda_{p_\star,q}\) and \(d_{\Delta,n}\) (= intermediate observation = the theoretical support for Claim 1’s 1-C).

7. Limitations and connection to the experimental claims

This theory corresponds to the two experimental claims as follows. Claim 1 (transfer, b=0): under linear operations and matched capacity, if the rank condition \(\row(\Psi)\subseteq\row(\Phi)\) holds then \(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0\) and composition aids forward transfer (Proposition 4). If, as with a three-body interaction, \(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\) remains from the kernel of the probe design, terminal-only cannot remove it zero-shot, and intermediate observation — which relaxes the depth limit — improves \(\Lambda_{p_\star,q}\) and \(d_{\Delta,n}\) to recover the conditions (Proposition 6). This is Claim 1. Claim 2 (active learning, b>0): even if identification fails, the direct active observation of the target terminal (full-support \(\lambda\) + correct posterior) recovers terminal-response consistency via Theorem 3 and Proposition 5, driving the error down faster through structure (Claim 2).
Limitations

(1) What vanishes is the latent-function variance \(v_n\), not the observation-predictive variance. (2) The main theorem relies on additive, conditionally unbiased, finite-variance observations; a general observation kernel requires local identifiability (a Fisher-information-type lower bound). (3) Noisy \(\MaxVar\) is not the main subject of this paper. (4) The consistency of approximate inference is not obtained automatically, and a fixed approximating family may under- or over-estimate the posterior variance. Claiming convergence to the best-in-class limit under misspecification requires separate assumptions such as a pseudo-true value, posterior concentration, and a misspecified-BvM type.
