Claim 1 · Composition helps transfer

Figure 3c — count identification and iterative extrapolation (C^k M O)

An experiment isolating, from the core of Claim 1, the question of whether the iteration count (count) of an operation can be extrapolated. The source is trained on 16 count probes (\(x\in[-1,1]^2\)) that each contain every operation at most once, and the target is evaluated on \(C^k M O\), which repeats the same operation C \(k=1,\dots,4\) times (\(x\in[0,1]^2\), the count being genuinely out of the training distribution). OFML-linear, which carries an explicit additive-affine structure, can extrapolate iteration by additively composing \(k\) times the increment of C that it has seen at most once in training (macro NRMSE 0.049), whereas the count-descriptor-based Shared-Ens, which carries no explicit composition rule, extrapolates freely beyond the training count and diverges with \(k\) (macro 1.175). This is a direct corollary of Proposition 4(iii)(iv), that additive-affine is globally uniquely determined by full-rank probes.

Goal (summary of the claim)

We validate Claim 1 by isolating identification/extrapolation with respect to the iteration count (count) of an operation. We show that even when the source contains each operation at most once, additive-affine composition can extrapolate the target iteration \(C^k M O\) (\(k=1..4\)) (OFML-linear, macro NRMSE 0.049). In contrast, Shared-Ens, which treats count without an explicit composition rule, diverges as \(k\) increases (macro 1.175), delineating the boundary of identifiability. This amounts to an empirical validation of Proposition 4(iii)(iv), that additive-affine is globally uniquely determined from full-rank probes.

Setup

On the synthetic-field benchmark we place two operation families: a linear family (_lin_truth, where each operation is a \(2\times2\) affine transform; additive-affine structure) and a nonlinear family (smooth non-affine operations _nl_truth). For each family, the source is trained on 16 count probes (systematically varying the number of occurrences of each operation, but with every operation appearing at most once = no iteration, \(x\in[-1,1]^2\)) at per_proto=32 points each with noise \(\sigma=0.02\), and the target is \(C^k M O\), which repeats the same operation C (\(k=1,2,3,4\) = C M O / C C M O / C C C M O / C C C C M O, \(x\in[0,1]^2\)), evaluated on n_test=80 points of noiseless ground truth. By shifting the input domain of source and target as \([-1,1]^2\to[0,1]^2\) and never showing count \(k\ge2\) during training, the count becomes genuinely out of distribution (extrapolation). We compare three surrogate models (OFML-linear / OFML-Ens / Shared-Ens) on identical data and average over 12 bench seeds as the primary statistical unit.

Note: the raw per-\(k\) curves are aggregated on the linear family (Figure 3c · Table 5). For the nonlinear family we report only the macro NRMSE as a cross-scale summary (Table 5b). All errors are NRMSE normalized by the per-\(k\), per-family scale on the test set; raw RMSE is not used. OFML-linear is inherently seed-independent under least-squares + affine estimation, but we average over 12 seeds to include the variability of observation-noise realizations.

All metrics are macro / per-\(k\) NRMSE. For each \(k\)'s target we normalize by its own scale \(s_k\) on the test set, \[ \NRMSE(k)=\sqrt{\mathrm{mean}_{x}\big(\mu_k(x)-f_k(x)\big)^2}\;\big/\;s_k,\qquad s_k=\sqrt{\mathrm{mean}_{x}\big(f_k(x)-\bar f_k\big)^2},\qquad \overline{\NRMSE}=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}\NRMSE(k),\ \ K=4. \] \(\mu_k\) is the model prediction, \(f_k\) is the ground truth of the target \(C^k M O\), and \(\bar f_k\) is the mean over the test set. Since \(s_k\) normalizes each \(k\)'s target to its own scale, errors can be compared fairly even when the amplitude changes as count grows. "count-blind" refers to the fact that Shared-Ens treats count as a mere input feature and imposes no additive-composition rule, so beyond the counts seen in training its predictions extrapolate freely.

Detailed setup (seed taxonomy · procedure · QC · limitations)
Seed taxonomy. bench_seed (12 of them) is the primary statistical unit and re-draws the true benchmark modules (the operation matrices and readouts of the linear/nonlinear families). We separate the derived streams: task=bench_seed, pool=bench_seed×7+1, noise=bench_seed×7+2, model=bench_seed. Cross-model comparisons are matched (paired) on the same bench_seed.

Procedure. For each family we train the source from 16 count probes (each operation ≤1 time) and evaluate \(C^k M O\) for \(k=1..4\). OFML-linear estimates the per-operation affine increment by least squares and constructs \(C^k\) analytically as the \(k\)-fold composition of the single C increment (training is deterministic; the seed only affects the noise realization). OFML-Ens / Shared-Ens are each trained once on the entire source of the family and evaluate all-\(k\) targets zero-shot (no target evidence = forward transfer only).

QC. The test lies at \(x\in[0,1]^2\), inside the source domain \([-1,1]^2\), but count \(k\ge2\) is outside training = genuine extrapolation. The test is noiseless (no train-on-test). The count descriptor of Shared-Ens uses the same implementation as the source and is byte-identical even under different orderings (order-blind = exposed separately in Figure 3b).

Limitations. The per-\(k\) raw curves are for the linear family only (Figure 3c). The nonlinear family gets a macro summary only (Table 5b). The extrapolation ability of OFML-linear depends on the additive-affine assumption and is not exact extrapolation on the nonlinear family. The NRMSE normalization scale \(s_k\) is an RMS approximation on the 80-point test set.

Discussion — how it supports the claim

This experiment isolates Claim 1's "composition helps transfer" in its sharpest form: extrapolation of count (iteration). Even though the source contains C at most once, OFML-linear can recover \(C^k M O\) at all \(k\) simply by additively composing the single C increment \(k\) times (macro 0.049). This is a direct corollary of the fact that the additive-affine structure is globally uniquely determined from full-rank probes (Proposition 4(iii)(iv)). In contrast, Shared-Ens treats count without an explicit composition rule, so beyond the training count its predictions lose their constraint and diverge with \(k\) — the presence or absence of a composition rule delineates the boundary of extrapolability. On the nonlinear family too the composition model is superior (0.391 vs 0.608), showing that the benefit of composition persists even when additivity holds only approximately.

For other cross-sections of forward transfer see Figure 3a (linear s0 parity) and Figure 3b (order: identification of ordering); for the rank audit of identifiability see the Φ/Ψ audit; and for the positioning under the cost index see Figure 2. count (this figure) and order (Figure 3b) form the "two axes of composition", and only when both are in place does the identification limit of terminal-only (Claim 1) become clear.

Note: limitations — the per-\(k\) raw curves are for the linear family only, and the nonlinear family is kept to a macro summary. The exact extrapolation of OFML-linear depends on the additive-affine assumption and does not reach genuinely nonlinear iteration (Table 5b indicates the ceiling). The divergence of Shared-Ens depends on the extrapolation-regime behavior of the count feature and its level may change with regularization or architecture, but as long as it lacks a composition rule the \(k\)-dependent degradation remains. The NRMSE normalization uses the scale on each \(k\)'s 80-point test set.

Correspondence with theory

This figure quantifies (iii)(iv) of Proposition 4: in the additive-affine family, the increment of each operation is globally uniquely determined from full-rank probes (the count basis of the source), and their sum extrapolates the iteration \(C^k\) to any \(k\) (\(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0\iff\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker\Psi\), with bounded condition number \(K\)). The floor-hugging of OFML-linear (Table 5) is the realization of this uniqueness, while the divergence of Shared-Ens corresponds to a hypothesis class without a composition rule being unable to identify count from the same probes. In settings where iteration is not identifiable (non-additive, insufficient probes), active observation at the target terminal becomes necessary (Theorem 3, Proposition 5).
