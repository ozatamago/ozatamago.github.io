Claim 1 · Composition helps transfer (identifiability audit)

Φ/Ψ Identifiability Audit — When Composition Transfers

An audit that measures the theory's rank condition directly on real data. For a synthetic affine-linear family with true parameters \(\theta\) (23 dimensions: five \(2\times2\) operation matrices plus a readout), let \(\Phi\) be the parameter Jacobian stacked over the source rows, let \(\Psi\) be the sensitivity of the target \((p_\star,q)\), and compute the target-relevant identification residual \(r^{\mathrm{id}}=\norm{\Psi(I-P_{\mathrm{row}}(\Phi))}_F/\norm{\Psi}_F\). Here \(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0\iff \ker\Phi\subseteq\ker\Psi\iff\) the target is identifiable from the source, and the condition number is \(K=\sigma_{\max}(\Psi)/\sigma_{\min}^{+}(\Phi)\). This directly turns the rank condition and condition number of Proposition 4 of the main theorem into a numerical quantity.

Goal (summary of the claim)

We directly verify the condition of Claim 1 — "recombination of identifiable operations \(\Rightarrow\) forward transfer succeeds" — through the rank relationship of \(\Phi/\Psi\) at the true \(\theta\). Whether \(r^{\mathrm{id}}\) is zero (i.e., whether \(\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker\Psi\)) determines whether that target can be identified from the source alone, and we show that this exactly predicts the forward transfer NRMSE of Figure 3a. A deterministic (seedless) theory–experiment bridge.

Setup

The synthetic affine-linear family _lin_truth (non-commuting operations A/B/C/D/M plus a linear readout, 23-dimensional parameters). For each scenario (a source/target pair), we form \(\Phi\) by stacking the parameter Jacobian at the true \(\theta\) over the source rows, and \(\Psi\) by stacking it over the target terminal \((p_\star,q)\). The metrics are \(r^{\mathrm{id}}=\norm{\Psi(I-P_{\mathrm{row}}(\Phi))}_F/\norm{\Psi}_F\) (where \(P_{\mathrm{row}}(\Phi)\) is the projection onto the row space of \(\Phi\)) and \(K=\sigma_{\max}(\Psi)/\sigma_{\min}^{+}(\Phi)\) (where \(\sigma_{\min}^{+}\) is the smallest singular value on \((\ker\Phi)^\perp\)). We also record the source rank per operation type \(\{A,B,C,D,M\}\) to see which operation directions are not excited in the source. The computation is purely linear-algebraic, with no randomness or training.

※ This audit is a direct implementation of Proposition 4 (\(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0\iff\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker\Psi\iff\row(\Psi)\subseteq\row(\Phi)\), \(K\le\norm{\Psi}_2/\sigma_{\min}^{+}(\Phi)\)). The old "identifiability↔capacity trade-off" figure has been replaced by this audit, which measures identifiability rigorously by rank.

Result

\(r^{\mathrm{id}}\) predicts the forward transfer NRMSE. The fully identifiable s0 (\(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0\)) lets OFML-linear recombine near-perfectly (NRMSE ≈0.04). Scenario s1, which uses operation D that is absent from the source, has the D direction fall into \(\ker\Phi\setminus\ker\Psi\), giving \(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0.414\) (source rank of operation D = 0). Scenario s4, with fewer source probes, under-excites B and C so the rank drops from 15→11; although the residual \(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0.167\) is small, its condition number \(K=178.5\) amplifies the error.

Discussion — how it supports the claim

This audit rigorously delimits, by rank, the scope of Claim 1 (the boundary between the conditions under which transfer succeeds and those under which it fails). If \(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0\) (identifiable), composition transfers (Claim 1, s0); if \(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\), it cannot be erased zero-shot under terminal-only (Claim 1, s1). Failure arises from the kernel of the probe design: in s1 operation D is absent from every source (\(\ker\Phi\setminus\ker\Psi\)), and the three-body interaction of s7 likewise produces \(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\) from the kernel of the monochromatic and dichromatic probes. Even with a small residual, s4 has a large \(K\), quantifying the intuition that in regimes where identification is tight, excess capacity destabilizes identification.

Correspondence with the theory

This audit is a faithful implementation of Proposition 4: \(r^{\mathrm{id}}=0\iff\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker\Psi\iff\row(\Psi)\subseteq\row(\Phi)\) (Proposition 4(i)), condition number \(K=\sigma_{\max}(\Psi)/\sigma_{\min}^{+}(\Phi)\le\norm{\Psi}_2/\sigma_{\min}^{+}(\Phi)\) (Proposition 4(ii)). Even in cases where identification breaks down (\(r^{\mathrm{id}}>0\)), terminal-response consistency can be recovered by directly and actively observing the target terminal (Theorem 3, Proposition 5).
