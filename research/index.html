Prediction, Active Learning, and Bayesian Optimization from Experimental Protocols: OFML / ProtocolBO and ProtocolUCB

Jun. 2024 – Aug. 2025, Jun. 2026 – Present
RIKEN BDR, Laboratory for Biologically Inspired Computing (PI: Koichi Takahashi); with Kazunari KaizuKobe, Japan

Developed a machine-learning system that predicts experimental outcomes from experimental protocols, written in the lab's Object-Flow Programming Language, and their conditions. Responsible for the whole project, from conceiving and designing the method through theory, implementation, numerical and physical experiments, and paper writing. Research Part-timer II; also affiliated with the RIKEN AGIS program (since Jun. 2026).

  • Operation-level surrogate model. Proposed OFML, which represents an experimental protocol as a typed directed acyclic graph and composes surrogate models shared by operation type [R1]. Implemented it with neural networks and, by learning from final measurements without labels for intermediate states, developed a way to predict the outcomes of unobserved protocols that recombine operations.
  • Theory and numerical experiments. Under stated assumptions, proved uniform convergence of the posterior mean under active learning by integrated variance reduction [R1], and convergence of the ProtocolUCB recommendation to the set of global maximizers [W1]. Also analyzed conditions for prediction on unobserved protocols, and in numerical experiments on synthetic protocols compared prediction error and mean protocol-wise simple regret across model-sharing and experiment-selection choices.
  • Closed loop on a physical instrument. Mapped OFML's operation-level surrogates to the operation modules of a Tecan Fluent and connected them to colored-water experiments. Ran a loop that repeats execution, measurement, and model updates, demonstrating that operation-level modeling, matched to the instrument's modules, links experiments and learning [R1].

Lipschitz Bandits: Cumulative Regret Analysis

Feb. 2026 – Present
University at Albany, SUNY, Department of Computer Science (Advisor: Chong Liu)Albany, NY, USA
  • Motivation and method. Examined the classical Piyavskii–Shubert algorithm from the viewpoint of cumulative regret, focusing on the problem that observation noise breaks its guaranteed upper bounds on function values. Proposed LiP-UCB, which handles noise through a statistical correction that propagates confidence intervals, estimated from nearby observations, to the whole domain via Lipschitz continuity [R2].
  • Cumulative regret analysis and lower bounds revisited. In the noiseless setting, proved a bound of 2L2L2L, independent of the horizon TTT, in one dimension and a bound of 3d L T1−1/d3\sqrt{d}\,L\,T^{1-1/d}3d​LT1−1/d for d≥2d\ge2d≥2. In the noisy setting, showed that the expected cumulative regret matches the lower bound up to logarithmic factors. Also identified a problem in an existing lower-bound proof and re-derived the lower bound under Gaussian noise [R2].
  • Numerical experiments. In the one-dimensional noiseless setting, tracked cumulative regret against the number of observations and confirmed that the Piyavskii–Shubert algorithm stays within the theoretical bound 2L2L2L. In the noisy setting, compared LiP-UCB with existing minimax-optimal methods and showed comparable performance in the settings tested [R2].

SPIN: Planning with Large Language Models

Aug. 2025 – May 2026
University at Albany, SUNY, with IBM (Collaborator: Dhaval Patel, IBM)Albany, NY, USA
  • Team leadership and collaboration. Led a student development team in an IBM-partnered capstone project and grew it into a research collaboration. Over about a year, researched and developed LLM agents for industrial asset operations together with their execution and evaluation infrastructure, building a system that covers prompt design, execution-trace logging, and structural validation and automatic evaluation of plans.
  • Motivation and method. Noting that external tool calls can dominate operating cost in industrial use, developed SPIN, which suppresses redundant execution and structural defects in plans. It validates and repairs a plan as a directed acyclic graph, evaluates the predicted outcome of a partial plan, and stops execution once it judges the result sufficient to answer [R3].
  • Numerical experiments. On 261 AssetOpsBench scenarios, reduced the average number of tool calls per run from 11.81 to 6.82 (about 42%) compared with an existing configuration. Also evaluated on 18 MCP-Bench tasks with two language models, confirming improvements in metrics for plan validity, tool selection, and dependency awareness [R3].

Laboratory Automation and Experiment Infrastructure

RIKEN BDR, Laboratory for Biologically Inspired Computing (PI: Koichi Takahashi)Kobe, Japan

Directed the agent to the parts to read and the points to check in the FluentControl device definitions and operation scripts and in the Maholo control software, and advanced the analysis while matching it against the physical layout and labware. Organized the operation specifications, communication protocols, and execution conditions, and led the development of SiLA 2 connectors (tecan-fluent, inheco-odtc) and execution scripts, building an environment in which the agent, with human approval, creates and runs procedures and checks instrument state without per-experiment procedure authoring in the GUI. On the Fluent, the connectors cover the pipetting arm, gripper, deck cameras and deck state, an Inheco ODTC thermal cycler, an Infinite 200 PRO plate reader, and a BioShake shaker.
  • Design and execution of physical tests, and procedure improvement. Defined the goals, experimental conditions, and the division of roles between people and the agent for the demos, and led supervised tests on the instruments: water dispensing, plate transport, and 30 thermal cycles on the Fluent, and nine steps including dispensing, sealing, mixing, and seal peeling in four protocols on the Maholo. When the agent failed, investigated the cause from instrument state and run logs and revised the procedure; for a plate left in the ODTC after a stop command failed following PCR, had the agent write a recovery procedure and retrieve the plate.
  • Control of related instruments and extension to lab automation. Developed a SiLA 2 connector for a Franka Research 3 arm that reads robot state, operates it through Desk, and captures camera images. Now working on automating E. coli transformation on the Fluent with the instrument-control infrastructure built here.

Gene Regulatory Network Inference from Single-Cell Omics

Feb. 2025 – Aug. 2025
The University of Osaka, PRIMe, Yachie Lab (PI: Nozomu Yachie)Osaka, Japan

Research Assistant.

  • Implementation and exploratory evaluation of expression-prediction models. Worked on predicting the per-cell expression of a single gene from genome-wide chromatin accessibility and gene expression. Implemented neural-network and Transformer models and, through training and prediction experiments, explored how far the two measurements together can predict expression.
